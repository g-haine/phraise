## Abstract
This paper proposes an adaptive Lyapunov–Hamiltonian control strategy for multi-cell (interleaved) boost converters in fuel-cell-based DC microgrids. Conventional cascade PI loops and small-signal designs struggle with constant-power loads (CPLs), whose negative incremental impedance can destabilize the DC bus. Using the port-Hamiltonian formalism, our approach links the Lyapunov function to the system Hamiltonian, achieving large-signal stability via energy shaping and damping assignment. We also introduce an adaptive inductor-current set-point generator requiring only three physically meaningful tuning gains. Hardware tests on a 2.5 kW, two-cell interleaved PEMFC boost converter—implemented on a 2.0 GHz dual-core 64-bit DSP—show precise DC-bus regulation, equalized current sharing, and strong dynamics under CPL disturbances. Overall, the method is scalable, robust, and physically interpretable for stabilizing FC-powered DC microgrids.
