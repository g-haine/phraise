## Abstract
Structural damage detection in flexible beam structures requires methods that combine physical interpretability with fault localization.This paper proposes an energy-based framework for damage detection, localization, and severity estimation in discrete Timoshenko cantilever beams, grounded in port-Hamiltonian system theory.The beam is formulated in explicit port-Hamiltonian form using finite elements, HRZ mass lumping, and Rayleigh proportional damping.The central contribution is the port-Hamiltonian Structural Consistency Residual, a powerbalance violation metric that decomposes analytically into element-level damage signatures, enabling localization through a matched-filter least-squares estimator without prior knowledge of damage location or magnitude.The framework is validated numerically on a cantilever beam across four configurations (uniform and non-uniform cross-section, each with 0% and 1% measurement noise) and fifty idealized stiffness-loss damage scenarios per configuration (10 elements × 5 severity levels, 2-30% stiffness reduction).Overall localization accuracy ranges from 86% (uniform, noiseless) to 66% (non-uniform, 1% noise), with exact identification reaching 100% at 30% severity for mid-span elements across all configurations.
