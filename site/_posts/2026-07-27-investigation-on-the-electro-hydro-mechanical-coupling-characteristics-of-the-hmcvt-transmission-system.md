## Abstract
To address transmission efficiency and stability issues in hydro-mechanical continuously variable transmissions (HMCVT) arising from the coupling of mechanical, hydraulic, and electrical domains, this study proposes a dynamic modeling and optimization strategy based on the Port-Hamiltonian formalism. A multidomain energy transfer model has been constructed to characterize the nonlinear coupling among the mechanical, hydraulic, and electrical subsystems. Numerical simulations and experimental analyses are conducted to evaluate the system’s dynamic response. The calibrated model further shows that, under the neutral swash-plate condition in the medium-speed range, the achieved peak efficiency reaches 89.52% at 1700 r/min and 853.17 N∙m. This high-efficiency condition is associated with a larger proportion of power being transmitted through the mechanical branch. Across several control strategies, the Port-Hamiltonian (PH) HMCVT model shows adaptability and supports physically interpretable analysis of power flow, efficiency variation, and transient response within the tested bench operating range.
