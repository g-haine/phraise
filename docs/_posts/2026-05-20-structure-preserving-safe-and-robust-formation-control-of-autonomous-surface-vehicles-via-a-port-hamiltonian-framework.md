## Abstract
This article proposes a robust and safe structure-preserving formation control framework for multiple autonomous surface vehicles (ASVs) within a port-Hamiltonian (PH) formulation. Different from conventional signal-based designs, the proposed approach regulates formation behaviors from an energy perspective, where the desired formation configuration is explicitly embedded into the closed-loop Hamiltonian and shaped via interconnection and damping assignment passivity-based control (IDA-PBC). This energy-centric design yields a physically interpretable and engineering-friendly controller, facilitating systematic tuning and practical implementation. To enhance robustness against time-varying environmental disturbances and model uncertainties, a structure-preserving observer is introduced to estimate and compensate lumped uncertainties without disrupting the intrinsic PH interconnection and damping structure. Furthermore, prescribed performance control is incorporated into the PH framework as a safety-oriented auxiliary mechanism, by transforming safety-constrained formation errors into PH-compatible states, thereby guaranteeing intervehicle collision avoidance during close-proximity operations while maintaining PH structure. Rigorous stability analysis establishes the input-to-state stability of the resulting closed-loop system. Numerical simulations and robot operating system (ROS)/Gazebo-based experiments validate the effectiveness and practicality of the proposed structure-preserving observer-based IDA-PBC (SPO-IDA) formation control strategy for safe and robust multi-ASV formation control in dynamic marine environments.
